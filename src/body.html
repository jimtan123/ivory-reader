How to read faster: landmarks, not mnemonics

Rhymes work at first but they make you count. Memorise a few landmark notes, then read neighbours as "one above" or "one below" the landmark.

Treble landmarksTreble G sits on the line the clef curls around. Middle C hangs one ledger line below. Top-space G is where the staff ends.
Bass landmarksBass F sits between the two dots of the clef. Middle C hangs one ledger line above. Bottom-line G is where the staff starts.
Treble lines, if you mustE G B D F. Spaces spell F A C E.
Bass lines, if you mustG B D F A. Spaces A C E G.

Left handRight hand

Fingers are numbered the same on both hands: thumb 1, index 2, middle 3, ring 4, little finger 5. Tap a key to hear it.

Before you play, check

  • Sit tall, forearms level with the keys, elbows slightly in front of your body.
  • Hand shape like holding a small orange. Knuckles up, fingers curved, play with fingertips.
  • Wrist level, not dropped below the keys and not raised.
  • Thumb plays on its side, on the corner of the nail. It does not lie flat.
  • Shoulders down. If they creep up, stop and shake out your arms.
  • Find middle C by feel: it is the white key just left of the two black keys nearest the maker's name.

How to train your ear

Hearing pitch is comparison, not magic. Almost nobody names a note out of silence; what you are building is the ability to hear how far one note sits from another. So the drills give you a bearing and ask about the distance from it.

Start hereHigher or lower with a wide gap. Then narrow the gap until one or two keys apart is still obvious.
Then intervalsFifths and octaves first, because they are hard to confuse with anything. Add thirds when those are automatic.
Then the noteWith Reference C first on, you hear the C, then the mystery note. Five keys is the beginner's range; widen it once you are past nine in ten.
Sing it backHum the note before you answer. Matching it with your voice tells you more than guessing does, and it is what makes the interval stick.
Little and oftenFive minutes a day beats an hour on Sunday. Ears change slowly and they do not cram.
Use the pianoWrong answers replay with the answer revealed. Play that interval on your own piano straight away, while it is still in your head.

How Listen, Play along and Wait mode differ

Listen plays the piece at the metronome tempo. Play along clicks through the piece at that tempo while you play; a key within a third of a beat counts as on time, and with one hand chosen the app plays the other. Wait mode lights the next keys and waits until you play every one of them. Moving line draws a line that rides the beat across the score in Listen and Play along; Wait mode has no clock, so it has no line. Slow is the point: the tempo is yours. Format

One line per setting, then one line per hand. Measures are separated by |.

title: Ode to Joy time: 4/4 key: C tempo: 72

RH: E4/q E4/q F4/q G4/q | G4/q F4/q E4/q D4/q

Note = pitch/length. Pitch: letter, optional # or b, octave (middle C is C4). Length: w whole, h half, q quarter, e eighth, s sixteenth, add . for dotted. Rest: r/q. Chord: [C4 E4 G4]/h. Tie to the next note: C4/h~. Notes without an accidental follow the key signature; write n for a natural, as in F4n. Triplet: 3(C4/e D4/e E4/e), three in the time of two. A second voice on the same staff, such as a note held under a moving line, goes on its own line, RH2: or LH2:, with the same bars and rests where it is silent.

Install Ivory Reader

Installing puts the app on your home screen, opens it without browser bars, and keeps it working with no signal.

On iPhone or iPad
  1. Open this page in Safari. Chrome on iPhone cannot install it.
  2. Tap the Share button, the square with an arrow pointing up.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add. The icon appears on your home screen.
On Android
  1. Open this page in Chrome.
  2. Tap the three dots menu.
  3. Tap Install app, or Add to Home screen.

Your saved pieces and progress live in this browser. Installing keeps them; clearing the browser data removes them.
